How the EMI is calculated

EMI stands for equated monthly instalment, the fixed amount you pay every month until a loan is repaid. This calculator uses the reducing-balance method:

EMI = P x r x (1 + r)^n / ((1 + r)^n - 1)

  • P is the loan amount.
  • r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate divided by 12 and by 100).
  • n is the number of monthly payments.

Shorter or longer term?

A longer term lowers each monthly payment but increases the total interest. A shorter term raises the monthly payment but reduces the extra amount you pay overall. The comparison table under the result shows terms from 3 months to 20 years side by side so you can see the trade-off for your own loan.

The payment schedule shows, month by month or year by year, how much of each payment goes to interest and how much reduces the loan.

Frequently asked questions

How much extra do I pay on a loan?

The extra amount is the total interest: total of all EMIs minus the loan amount. The calculator shows it in money and as a percentage of the loan.

Does a longer loan term save money?

It lowers the monthly payment but usually costs more in total, because interest is charged for more months.

What is the difference between flat and reducing-balance rates?

A flat rate charges interest on the original loan amount for the whole term. A reducing-balance rate charges interest only on what you still owe. This tool uses reducing balance, so a lender quoting a flat rate will have a higher effective cost than the same number here.

Does the result include fees?

No. Processing fees, insurance and taxes vary by lender and are not included.
